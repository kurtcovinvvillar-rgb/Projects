The Game Called Chess
By: Kurt Covin V. Villar - kcvvillar@pcu.edu.ph




About the Game

Chess is a board game for two players, played on a square board consisting of 64 squares arranged in an 8×8 grid. The players, referred to as "White" and "Black", each control sixteen pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights, and eight pawns, with each piece type having a different pattern of movement. An enemy piece may be captured (removed from the board) by moving one's own piece onto the square it occupies. The object of the game is to "checkmate" (threaten with inescapable capture) the enemy king. There are also several ways a game can end in a draw.



Relevance

Chess remains significantly relevant today because its simple rules give way to infinite complexities, making it a powerful tool for cognitive development, a thriving digital esport, and a timeless social equalizer that builds critical decision-making skills in an increasingly automated world. Despite being over 1,500 years old, chess is experiencing a massive global resurgence. It remains deeply relevant because it bridges ancient strategy with modern technology, serving as a powerful tool for personal growth, digital entertainment, and social connection.



Benefits

Playing Chess acts as a rigorous mental workout that actively exercises both hemispheres of the brain to sharpen long-term cognitive skills. By forcing players to anticipate their opponent's actions, memorize complex opening theories, and recognize tactical patterns, the game significantly enhances problem-solving abilities and working memory. This intense, sustained focus serves as a powerful antidote to modern digital distractions, helping to improve attention spans and cultivate deep concentration. Furthermore, the complex mental flexibility required on the board provides lasting health advantages; regular play has been shown to build cognitive reserve, which can protect older adults from mental decline and help postpone the onset of conditions like dementia.
